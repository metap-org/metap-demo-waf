C1 — SYSTEM CONTEXT

Who and what touches the platform

One box for the whole system. Nothing inside it is shown at this level — only who uses it and what it talks to outside its own boundary.

C2 — CONTAINER

What's deployed inside the boundary

Every box here is a separately deployable thing — a process or a datastore — grouped by the plane that owns it. Plum boxes are proposed containers this system needs to add, not containers that exist today.

C3 — COMPONENT

Inside the two hand-built containers

Zooming further into waf-graphql-gateway, zones-service, etc. wouldn't earn its place here — they're generic entity CRUD, the same shape for every entity, nothing about their insides is specific to this product. waf-config-distributor and waf-edge are the two containers actually hand-written for this system, so they're the two worth a component diagram.

What this view leaves out on purpose: C4's own convention stops component diagrams at containers worth the detail — the generic CRUD containers (zones-service, scanning-service, alerting-service, the gateway) are identical in shape to each other and to every other metap-go service; a C3 for one would just be a C3 for all of them. Code-level flow (Redis key names, poll intervals, step-by-step compile logic) lives one level down from here — the right call when reviewing implementation, not when reviewing architecture.
